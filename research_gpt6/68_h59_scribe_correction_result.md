## Why the test is blocked
Several loci do show visually unusual superposition, darkening, doubled strokes, or probable overwriting. However, the available digital pixels do not uniquely determine the earlier glyph state. In other cases the lower layer is obscured, the contrast is too low, or reinforcement/re-inking cannot be distinguished from a content-changing correction.

Selecting a preferred `before` form from those possibilities would violate the preregistration by inventing invisible stroke history or using post-hoc paleographic judgment.

Therefore the predictive experiment is stopped at the blind data-audit gate.

## Interpretation
H59 establishes a useful negative design result: the small set of publicly visible apparent emendations is not presently sufficient for a clean confirmatory natural-experiment test of Voynich production grammar when a unique pre-correction state is required.

This does **not** imply that the manuscript contains no corrections, that the text is meaningless, or that correction processes carry no information. A future replication could become executable if multispectral, raking-light, microscopic, or other authoritative imaging recovers earlier stroke states for at least six independent events. Such future evidence must be audited before statistical scoring under a new preregistration or an explicitly frozen extension.
